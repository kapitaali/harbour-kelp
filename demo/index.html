Kelp

a web page shared from someone else's phone

You are reading this from your own device

This page was not downloaded from a website. It arrived as a kelp:// link, was fetched object by object, and is being served to this browser from 127.0.0.1 on the device in your hand.

“It is not on the internet. It is on the two phones at the ends of the link.”

How it got here

  1. Sealed. The sender's phone encrypted this page and cut it into addressable objects. The key went into the link's fragment, after the # — a part a browser never sends anywhere.
  2. Checked. Each object was verified against its SHA-256 address before it was kept. One altered byte and the object is refused, not used.
  3. Fetched. Your device asked the sender's phone for the objects it needed, following the manifests inside the bundle until nothing was missing.
  4. Served. What you see is served from a loopback address on your own device — the same mechanism the sender used to preview it before sharing it.

What this page cannot do

  • It cannot reach the internet. No CDN, no analytics, no fonts from anywhere, no invisible pixel reporting that you opened it. Everything here — the stylesheet, the leaf above, this text — came out of the bundle.
  • It cannot see where you are, who you are, or what else is on the device.
  • It cannot follow you after you leave. Nothing was written: no file, no cache entry, no record in the app's storage. The copy you are reading lives in memory and is dropped when the preview session ends.

What the sender learns

Serving the page was the sender's phone answering a request, so it saw that something connected, from which address, and how much was transferred — one line in its log, a bare address:port, not a name and not a location. The rest of the link told that log nothing: the address the page was served from and the fact that it is a page travelled sealed inside the link, readable only with the key from its fragment. The only part in the open is the bundle's identity at the front of the link, and that is just a hash of the contents.

A demonstration page for Kelp, a peer-to-peer file and page sharer for Sailfish OS. Three files, no external references, 100% of it from the bundle.
